# Research Limitations and Future Improvements

## 1. Limitations of the Study

Our research used an existing dataset of 300 university students. 
One important limitation is the unequal distribution of participants: 277 students reported using electronic devices before bedtime, 
while only 23 reported not using them. This imbalance makes it difficult to compare the two groups reliably.

Another limitation is that sleep quality was measured through self-reported categories rather than the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI). 
The dataset also did not include exact screen-time duration or detailed information about device activities.
Because this was a cross-sectional study, the results show associations rather than cause-and-effect relationships.

## 2. Interpretation of the Findings

Our analysis did not find a statistically significant association between device use before bedtime and sleep quality.
However, this does not prove that electronic devices have no influence on sleep.
We found that higher stress levels were associated with lower odds of reporting good sleep quality (OR = 0.76, p = 0.001). 
This suggests that stress is an important factor to consider when studying students' sleep habits.

## 3. Recommendations for Future Research

In future research, we would collect primary data through our own student survey. 
We would include questions about daily screen time, device use during the hour before bedtime, sleep duration, stress levels and sleep quality using the PSQI.

We would also aim to recruit a larger and more balanced sample of students with different bedtime device-use habits. 
This could help us understand the relationship between digital habits, stress and sleep quality more accurately.

## 4. Conclusion

The study provides useful evidence about the relationship between stress and sleep quality among university students. 
However, more detailed data are needed before drawing strong conclusions about electronic-device use before bedtime.
